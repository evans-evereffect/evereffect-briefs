Discovery script — tuned to what we now know

v2 restructures this tab into a real call flow: open with the specific finding, qualify with questions tied to what we found, handle the objections we can already see coming, then close on a named ask.

1. Open with the specific finding

Lead with the specific finding, not the general pitch "I watched a couple episodes of Covered by NewLeaf before this call. Real substance, and you're clearly not new to being on camera. What I noticed is the view counts have actually been trending down over the last several months even as you've kept posting — that usually means it's a discoverability problem, not a content problem."

2. Qualifying questions — tied to what we found (new in v2)

AskWhy we're asking itListen for
"You've never run paid ads on Google or Meta — was that a deliberate call, or just never gotten to?" Tied to the confirmed zero-paid-spend finding. Deliberate (organic-first philosophy, budget exists but unallocated) vs. never prioritized (harder, more education-heavy sell).
"Besides you, who else would weigh in on bringing in outside help for the channel — RD, Laura, anyone else?" Tied to the real leadership team (RD Patel CFO, Laura Gaitan ops) — don't assume Sean is the only stakeholder just because he's CEO. A named co-decision-maker vs. a confirmed sole owner of this decision.
"How are you currently finding out whether an episode landed or not — views, comments, something else?" Tied to the missing schema/OG/meta-description layer found in the SEO audit — there's likely no real measurement layer either. No real analytics in place (bigger need) vs. an existing process we'd be improving.
"Is there a reason this is coming up now, or did the InMail just land at a good moment?" Tied to the Timing block — no dated trigger event was found, so this asks directly instead of guessing. A real reason (real urgency) vs. no particular reason (longer sales cycle, more of an education play).
"Do you track what Warrantech or the bigger players like Asurion are doing on YouTube?" Tied to the Competitive Landscape tab — tests whether he already knows he's ahead of his old employer. Awareness of the competitive gap (easier close, he already feels the stakes) vs. no awareness (this becomes news he needs to hear).

3. Anticipated objections

"We don't have budget for a new vendor relationship right now." Reframe as fixing what already exists, not adding new spend: the gaps found (metadata, titles, descriptions) are fix-the-existing-channel work, not new production or new ad spend — the exact opposite of a new recurring cost.
"Our reviews (Yelp, the claims complaints) mean we shouldn't draw more attention to ourselves right now." Counter with the real, bigger picture: 4.2/5 across 3,536 Google reviews, with the owner actively responding to the negative ones. The Yelp sample is small and skewed; the real signal is strong.

4. Close: recommended package & next step (new in v2)

Ask for a scoped technical audit, not an open-ended engagement "If it's useful, I can put together a specific list of what I'd fix first on the channel and the site — titles, descriptions, schema — no new video required to start. I'd want 30 minutes with whoever actually touches the channel day to day to confirm a few things before I write it up."
